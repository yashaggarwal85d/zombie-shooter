# Game Vision

Version 0.1 · 22 October 2026 · Status: **draft for Yash's approval** (Stage B). Working title: none yet.

This is the one-page "why" for the game. When a feature, asset or idea is in doubt, check it against the pillars below. Details are in [GAME_DESIGN_DOCUMENT.md](GAME_DESIGN_DOCUMENT.md); decisions and their reasons are in [DECISION_LOG.md](DECISION_LOG.md).

## The pitch

> **Hold the house. Keep her frozen. Every death rewinds the night, but you remember what you learned.**

A side-on horde-defence shooter set in one building. Your infected spouse sleeps in a cold chamber at its centre. The chamber's hum keeps her alive and calls the dead. You are infected too. By day you prepare and test her cure on yourself. By night you hold the house. When you fall, the night starts over, and only what you learned survives.

## The feeling

Devotion under hopeless odds, and the cost of trying again. Quiet dread in the dark, then the noise you chose to make.

## Pillars

1. **Defend her, not yourself.** The chamber is the centre of the screen and of every decision. If a feature doesn't change how you protect her, it doesn't belong.
2. **Every sound is a choice.** The dead go to the loudest thing in the house. Loud is powerful and dangerous; quiet is safe and slow. Weapons, traps and mutations all trade on noise.
3. **Knowledge is the only thing you keep.** Each loop changes what you know (recipes, findings, her story), never your resources or stats.
4. **Light in the dark.** A silhouette world lit by one cold light. Atmosphere comes from shape, fog and light, never at the cost of seeing the danger.
5. **Small and sharp.** One house, few systems, each one deep. A small, exceptional game beats a large unfinished one.

## Who it's for

Players of prep-then-defend games (Dome Keeper, Thronefall, The Last Spell, Kingdom) who want more meaningful choices between waves, and value atmosphere. They buy small, replayable PC games at $10–18 and play on Steam Deck. Narrative-indie players are a secondary audience.

## What it is not

- Not a survivors-like or bullet heaven. No auto-attack, no screen-filling swarms.
- Not an open-world survival sim. You never leave the house.
- Not a metroidvania. Visual feel only is drawn from Hollow Knight and Silksong; no characters, plot, scenes, enemies, music, assets, structure or combat.
- Not The Last of Us. Tone only; no characters, plot or scenes.
- No multiplayer, procedural worlds, dialogue trees, branching story or voiced dialogue.

## Game one in numbers (targets, D5–D8)

- One arena, 3–4 enemies plus a boss night, 10–12 weapon components, 4–5 traps, 6–8 experiments, about 20 story fragments and one ending.
- Runs of 20–35 minutes; the ending at about 3–5 hours; replay beyond.
- 600–900 hours of development. Windows on Steam at $9.99–12.99, then Android and iOS together as a premium port.
- Commercial target: ship, recoup costs, 2,000–5,000 units. The long-term US$1M goal is net revenue across several games.
